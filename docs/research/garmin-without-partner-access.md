# Garmin without partner access: how a ride gets to Kadence by itself

_Research read 2026-09-29 in eight parallel tracks: Garmin's own programme and terms, the EU Data Act, the unofficial Garmin Connect libraries, Connect IQ, the local paths (USB, Bluetooth, the phone), relay services, other bike computers, what comparable platforms did, and Kadence's own code. Sources are the vendors' own documents, API specifications, repositories and forums; each is named where it is used. Read a second time against the source, the same day: Garmin's programme FAQ and application form, intervals.icu's API specification, API terms, webhook notes and privacy policy, Tredict's API documentation, terms and privacy statement, Gadgetbridge's device page, export page and changelog, Hammerhead's developer article and OpenAPI file, COROS's MCP article and server metadata. Everything else was read once. Nothing was tested against a live account or a device, no account was registered anywhere, and the web search allowance ran out midway in every track, so "none found" is weak evidence. A second round the same day went back to the claims of the kind "none found" with searches to spend; what it changed is marked **second round**. Tredict and intervals.icu answered in writing the same day; what they said is marked **answered**. Not legal advice. Everything marked **unverified** could not be confirmed against a source._

**Short answer.** The belief that others have solved this without special access from Garmin is half right. Those who did it use the unofficial login: the rider's Garmin password, against Garmin's terms, dark for 16 to 58 days after 17 March 2026, refused from data-centre addresses, and ending in a token that opens the rider's whole Garmin account. That is not for a tool that holds health data about minors. The official door is shut as well: Garmin has taken no new applicants since 25 March 2026, and bought TrainingPeaks on 22 July 2026. What does work, and stands on written terms, is of two kinds. A **relay** that already holds Garmin access and hands the original file on: Tredict (German, EU-hosted, riders from 14) and intervals.icu (free, webhooks, but by its own policy not for anyone under 18). And **local paths** around Garmin's cloud: the cable and a folder picker, or Gadgetbridge on Android. Every one of them needs the same thing Kadence lacks today: a way to receive a file without a browser session. The recommendation is to build that door first (an upload key per rider), then a relay adapter as the automatic path for the rider with an Edge and a phone, and to send Garmin a Data Act request in the meantime. Among other bike computers, Hammerhead Karoo is the most open and fits the adapter Kadence already has.

**Since then.** The door is built: an upload key per rider, the bike computer read as a folder, and a share from the phone (ADR 0029, docs/upload-keys.md). Tredict and intervals.icu have answered in writing (§3.1): Tredict offers the rider's own key rather than OAuth, and intervals.icu leaves its policy on riders under 18 as it is. What is left to ask Tredict is whether a rider needs a paid account.

## 1. The two doors that are shut

### 1.1 Garmin's own programme

- **Business only, and closed.** The FAQ says access is free but "only for business use" (<https://developer.garmin.com/gc-developer-program/program-faq/>). The application form carries a block named "System Maintenance - Garmin Connect Developer Program" with start date 2026-03-25 and the text "Stay tuned for more updates on the program." (<https://www.garmin.com/en-US/forms/GarminConnectDeveloperAccess/>). Garmin's answers to applicants on its forum: "new access requests are temporarily paused", "no projected re-opening date", "no notification list" (forums.garmin.com threads 434798, 434542, 436226, April to September 2026). Existing partners are unaffected.
- **Who was turned away in 2026** (the5krunner.com, 2026-09-14): CycleLytic applied 2 March and was still waiting on 11 September; Blocks applied 16 July and was told no new connections, and was "routed through intervals.icu as a bridge, or given FIT files"; Hevy was pointed to Connect IQ.
- **The agreement** (FRM-0952 Rev. B, PDF of 2026-03-03) has no room for open source. The key may not be shared: a licensee may not "sell, lease, share, transfer, or sublicense the License Key" (§5.2), so every self-hosted installation would need its own approved legal entity. Use "to compete with Garmin" is forbidden (§5.2), Garmin may end the licence with 30 days' notice (§9.2) and may introduce fees (§10.1). The maintainer of python-garminconnect on his own application: "since you get keys, you cannot have it open-sourced, it was denied" (issue 362, 2026-05-07).
- **One non-profit was let in, in 2020** (**second round**): Quantified Flu, MIT-licensed and run by the Open Humans Foundation, wrote on 2020-04-02 "We now got access to Garmin's API and the documentation" (github.com/OpenHumans/quantified-flu issue 27). That was an organisation's hosted service with its key in the deployment's settings, not a code base others run. RADAR-base and Open Wearables, both open source, make every installation bring its own credentials, and Open Wearables' guide requires "a legal entity (company, university, hospital, research institution, etc.)".
- **What the programme would give**, for the day it reopens: original FIT files, push or ping/pull, backfill capped at five years (Runalyze, 2026-09-17). Showing a rider's data to a coach is allowed "in compliance with each End User's consent" (§4.1) with Garmin attribution (§6.4). AI use needs a transparency statement and explicit, revocable consent (§15.10).
- **Direction.** Connect+ subscription (2025-03-27), attribution rules reaching exports, APIs and AI output (brand guidelines 2025-06-30), the freeze (2026-03-25), TrainingPeaks bought (2026-07-22). Garmin now owns the leading coaching platform; a friendlier programme for a small open coaching tool is unlikely.

Kadence's Garmin adapter (ADR 0018) is complete against fakes and stays dormant until this changes.

### 1.2 The unofficial login

This is how the projects that "solved it" did it: Endurain, garmin-grafana, GarminDB, the community Home Assistant integration, Dreeve's sidecar.

- **It broke, badly, last winter.** On 2026-03-17 Garmin put Cloudflare bot protection in front of its login. garth, the library the others stood on, was given up ten days later: "New logins will not work" (<https://github.com/matin/garth/discussions/222>). Time to work again: python-garminconnect 16 days, Home Assistant 24, Endurain 31 (and every user had to link again), garmin-grafana 34, GarminDB 58, two exporters never. Further breaks on 2026-06-02 and 2026-07-21, and on 2026-09-28 a report that a valid token is refused without imitating a browser's TLS fingerprint (python-garminconnect issue 444, one report, **unverified**).
- **It is refused from servers.** Same code, same account, 2026-08-11: a Hetzner address got Cloudflare's 403, a home connection passed (Sinfjell/garmin-mcp). Azure and Railway report the same. Kadence runs on Hetzner.
- **The token is not a grant, it is the account.** It is Garmin's own app session: read, upload, edit and delete, over sleep, HRV, cycle and location as well as rides. Logging out "does not revoke a token that has already been issued by Garmin". Child and family accounts are not supported by the library.
- **It is against the terms, and the law adds to it.** Garmin's Terms of Use, effective 2026-04-01, list as a violation "Using any process, whether automated or manual, that accesses, copies, or scrapes content from the Site through any means not purposely made available", and say "You must not share your account credentials with any third party." Neither sentence was in the text of 2021 (**second round**). Garmin's support to a user on 2026-03-24: "Garmin's terms of use prohibit using an unauthorized third-party service to retrieve data from Garmin Connect." The Data Act, the same regulation that gives the rider a right to their data, says a third party "shall not use coercive means or abuse gaps in the technical infrastructure" (art. 5(5)); in Denmark that is punishable by fine (lov nr. 1618 of 2025-12-16, §10).
- **Accounts have been locked over it** (**second round**; the first round found none). "my account got deactivated - twice", and Garmin's support "concluded that I should disable the program because it makes calls to their SSO service"; unlocking took a support ticket (github.com/pe-st/garmin-connect-export issue 60, with the same from others in 2021, 2022 and April 2025). A report from 2026 has refusals growing into a locked account. No legal letter and no takedown was found: GitHub's archive of notices, about 22,000 of them to 2026-09-25, does not name Garmin, and the maintainer of python-garminconnect, asked on 2026-05-07, knew of none.
- **Those who started here left.** tapiriik after an incident on 2019-06-11 that put a test account's rides into users' accounts; Smashrun when the path "threatens to disappear entirely".

A hosted Kadence doing this would be impersonating Garmin's app on behalf of minors and holding tokens over their whole accounts. Garmin's partner agreement also forbids a licensee to "request or otherwise attempt to obtain the End User's Garmin login credentials" (§15.8), so shipping it could cost a later official application.

## 2. The lever: the Data Act and GDPR article 20

- **The right exists on paper.** Regulation (EU) 2023/2854 has applied since 2025-09-12. Art. 5(1): on request "by a user, or by a party acting on behalf of a user", the data holder makes the data available to a third party "without undue delay … free of charge to the user … and, where relevant and technically feasible, continuously and in real-time". Art. 8–9: fair and non-discriminatory terms, and cost-only compensation when the recipient is a small enterprise. The Commission's FAQ (v1.4, 2026-01-22): raw data generated after 2025-09-12, and the third party must be in the EU.
- **Garmin is in scope by its own account.** It publishes Data Act notices for "Cycling Computers and Tacx Trainers" and for wearables, format "FIT and JSON", last updated 2025-09-12. They point to the rider's own account management and say nothing about third parties.
- **Others answered differently.** Polar and Wahoo point to their APIs; Suunto takes requests for "non-integrated partners" through support; COROS names GDPR and the Data Act as the reason it opened self-service access.
- **Nobody has won a feed this way yet.** No case was found of a third party obtaining wearable data under art. 5, in either round: no authority publishes one, no court has ruled, and a law firm's survey of 2026-06-18 says "no major enforcement actions have been reported to date" (Wilson Sonsini). GDPR art. 20 reaches direct transmission only "where technically feasible" and has given nobody a continuous feed.
- **Garmin's notice leaves out what it must say** (**second round**). A provider of a related service tells the user "how the user can request that the data are shared with a third party and, where applicable, end the data sharing" (art. 3(3)(f)). Garmin's notices name no form, no address, no portal and no API for it. That is worth citing in the request.
- **What is covered** is data generated after 2025-09-12 (Digitaliseringsstyrelsen's FAQ).
- **What Kadence would owe** (art. 6): agreed purposes only, erasure when no longer needed, no passing on. Health data means explicit consent.
- **Enforcement.** Digitaliseringsstyrelsen in Denmark (lov nr. 1618), with forms on borger.dk and virk.dk. Garmin's controller for the EEA is Garmin Würzburg GmbH, Leightonstr. 7, 97074 Würzburg; the Bundesnetzagentur says a case across borders goes to the authority where the data holder is established, so a Danish complaint would probably be passed on to Germany, whose own law on it came into force on 2026-05-30 (**second round**).

This costs a letter and may take months. It is the only route that could open Garmin's own feed to every EU self-hoster without the partner programme.

- **intervals.icu stands on written terms.** API terms effective 2025-10-23: a "non-exclusive, worldwide, royalty-free, perpetual license to access and use the API for any lawful purpose, including commercial use", on one condition: "If your application displays information derived from Garmin-sourced data, you must display attribution to Garmin". Webhooks `ACTIVITY_UPLOADED` and `ACTIVITY_ANALYZED`, verified by a shared secret in the body; limits of 100 requests per user per day for an app, 5,000 a day for a key. The self-hosted tool Tempo already reads Garmin rides this way (a 15-minute poll with the rider's key, merged 2026-09-17).
- **Tredict fits the riders, and has been closing.** Fourteen and up, German, on EU soil. It has no webhooks, so Kadence would ask every quarter of an hour. **Second round:** its terms since 2026-06-04 say "Tredict also reserves the right to restrict read access for external interfaces if no paid period is active", naming the calendar feed, the Personal API and the MCP server; its price page now says "Full access is required for detailed data and advanced functions using the Tredict MCP Server or the Personal API", a sentence that was not there on 2026-06-30; the trial went from three months to two. OAuth is not named, but it shares its endpoints with the Personal API, and the file endpoint lists no "payment required". Count on USD 49 a rider a year until Tredict says otherwise in writing. Tredict publishes no terms for API clients and says nothing about Garmin's data; it calls itself "an official Garmin integration partner".
- **What comes through a relay is Garmin's filtered file** (**second round**). intervals.icu, 2026-03-12: "Garmin now does some filtering on the files they return via the API. The file is not the same as what you can download manually from Garmin Connect". Kadence's decoder has to read those, and the same ride uploaded by hand is another file with the same start.
- **Nobody at intervals.icu has said anything about riders under 18** (**second round**, some twenty searches of its forum). Coaches of U15 to U19 riders write there openly, and the founder has answered one without a word about age. The policy stands as written.
- **Two German platforms are gone.** Velo Hero and Trainingstagebuch.org "ceased operations on May 1, 2026", and their data with them. A relay is a small company; the file upload is what stays.
- **The catch is Garmin's.** A partner may not build "a Licensee application programming interface to provide End User Data to third-party websites, software applications, platforms, services, or products that have not been approved by Garmin in writing" (§5.2(o)), while the brand guidelines regulate exactly that onward flow: "receiving systems must preserve attribution, and this should be outlined in a partner sublicense." So it is forbidden by default and allowed with approval and attribution. Whether either relay holds that approval is not published. Every relay lives at Garmin's discretion, and manual upload stays the fallback.
- **Aggregators** fail dogmas 1 and 2: a monthly fee per installation and no self-hosting. Open Wearables, the open one, needs each deployment's own Garmin keys.
- **Data protection.** The relay is a service the rider chooses, a controller in its own right, and belongs in the second table of `docs/subprocessors.md` before it is used. The rider and the guardian are told who the relay is and where it works, that it receives what the rider ticks on Garmin's consent screen while Kadence reads rides only, that deleting in Kadence deletes nothing at the relay, and how to disconnect. Kadence does not send a rider to a service whose terms exclude them.

### 3.1 What the two relays answered, in writing, on 2026-09-29

Both answered by e-mail the same day they were asked. The answers are given here in Kadence's words, not theirs: a private letter is not quoted in a public repository without its writer's yes.

**Tredict** (Felix Gertz, a quarter of an hour after the question):

- Kadence should use the Personal API, with a token each rider makes, rather than an OAuth client; an OAuth connection can come later if Kadence grows. Tredict's documentation says the same: the Personal API is "the recommended choice for new or small apps without a larger user base yet, like coaching apps. Each user generates their own token and enters it into the app (bring your own key, BYOK). As the app owner you then hold the tokens of all your users and have a special duty of care when storing them" (tredict.com/skills/setup.md).
- The rider makes the token in Tredict under Settings, "Personal API", and chooses its scopes; `activityRead` is all Kadence needs. It is a bearer token that does not run out. The endpoints are the OAuth ones: the list is `GET /api/oauth/v2/activityList`, newest first, with `startDate`, `endDate` and a page size of 50 to 1,000; the file is `GET /api/oauth/v2/activity/file/{id}`, "the file as Tredict received it from the source platform". A wrong or deleted token answers 401, a missing scope 403. Downloads are limited to ten a second by address.
- This suits self-hosting better than OAuth would: no installation needs keys of its own, so the relay works on every Kadence the day it is built (dogmas 1 and 2).
- Not answered yet: whether a rider without a paid account can read the list and the file through the Personal API; whether asking every quarter of an hour is welcome; whether Tredict has conditions for rides that came from Garmin; what riders and parents should be told. A reply asking the four again is written.

**intervals.icu** (David Tinker, two hours after the question):

- On riders under 18 he is not sure how it would work legally, nor how a parent's consent could be obtained, and has left the policy as it is. Riders under 18 are therefore not welcomed in writing, and Kadence offers intervals.icu to riders of 18 and over only, and says why.
- Garmin currently asks a receiving application for attribution and nothing else. That is the first written word on §5.2(o) from a partner.
- Every installation would register its own OAuth app, since a shared client id and secret could be used by anyone; or the rider pastes their own API key, as with Tredict.

## 4. Local paths: around Garmin's cloud

### 4.1 The cable

- Garmin moved its newer devices from plain storage to MTP. **MTP:** Edge 540, 550, 840, 850, 1040, 1050, Explore 2, MTB, and most current watches. **Plain storage** (by absence from Garmin's list, an inference): Edge 130 and 130 Plus, 520, 530, 830, 1030. Rides are in `Garmin/Activities`.
- A phone reads plain storage; it does not read an MTP Garmin reliably. Garmin support on an Edge 1050 and a Pixel: "The Edge can only be connected to a PC or via Bluetooth with the Garmin Connect app".
- A browser can pick a folder: `showDirectoryPicker` in Chrome on a computer and, since version 132, on Android; `<input webkitdirectory>` also in Safari on iOS from 18.4. An installed Chrome app keeps the permission.
- So "plug in, open Kadence, press once" can be built for the older Edges on every platform, and for the newer ones on a computer. Whether an Edge 530 mounts on an iPhone needs a device on the table (**unverified**).

### 4.2 Gadgetbridge, Android only

- An open-source (AGPL-3.0) replacement for the Garmin Connect app that fetches the original FIT over Bluetooth. Since 0.92.0: "Auto FIT export … the original file is exported unchanged", and "Add activity upload to Endurain / Wanderer". Since 0.94.0 (2026-09-17) the device starts the sync itself. A general upload layer where a new service costs "about four files" is an open pull request (6857, 2026-09-26).
- It lists 24 Edge models, but "Support for some of these devices is recent, and still experimental": fetching rides is confirmed for Edge 130 Plus, 1040 and Explore 2; the 520, 530, 830, 1030, 550 and 850 were "added without access to the device".
- It takes the place of Garmin Connect ("make sure that the official app is not running"), comes from F-Droid rather than Google Play, and loses LiveTrack and Garmin's own relay to other services. Nothing like it exists for iPhone.

### 4.3 On an iPhone

- **One app does it, by logging in to Garmin on the phone** (**second round**). RunGap (CTRL-N ApS, Skødstrup; iOS only; DKK 129 a year) imports from Garmin Connect and, since version 26.5 of 2026-03-17, exports to a custom webhook, unattended when iOS lets it run in the background. It reaches Garmin with the rider's own Garmin login, held on the phone, and that broke on 2025-11-20, 2026-01-05 and 2026-03-27. It sends a form signed with a shared secret, not a key in a header. This is the unofficial login moved from a server to the rider's pocket: the rider's choice and the rider's account. Kadence builds nothing for it (§9, point 7).
- **Nothing else was found.** No app on iOS talks to a Garmin over Bluetooth without Garmin Connect. HealthFit reads Apple Health only. No Shortcuts automation fires on a new file; the ones that run without asking are about time, place, power, an app opened or closed, and the like.
- **The cable is proven on an iPad, not on an iPhone.** "can fully browse my Edge 830 from the Files app" (an iPad Pro, 2020); an Edge 530 on a Lightning iPad needed a powered hub. No report of an Edge on an iPhone was found.

### 4.4 What does not carry a ride

- **Apple Health and Health Connect.** Garmin writes no route and no power to either; to Apple Health only "high/low HR values", and "Connect will not appear as an option in Apple Health if you only have an Edge cycling computer registered".
- **The Garmin Connect app** exports no file; only the website's "Export Original" does, and Kadence reads that zip already (ADR 0022). Garmin's account-level export is a zip of original files by e-mail within 48 hours to 30 days: a way to bring a rider's history in, not a feed.
- **Web Bluetooth** (Chrome only, foreground only, collides with Garmin Connect) and **ANT-FS** (watches of 2009–2013) are not worth building.
- **An installed web app can receive a shared file** on Android (`share_target`, Chrome 76); Safari has no support (WebKit bug 194593, still open).

## 5. Connect IQ: an app on the Edge itself

- **It cannot send the ride.** "IQ apps can't read FIT (or any) files." A request carries a small JSON dictionary, normally through the phone over Bluetooth at 400–800 bytes a second. Four hours at one sample a second is about 300 KB against 128 KB of storage and 32 KB of background memory on an Edge 530.
- **It can do two smaller things, with nobody's approval but the store's review.** _A summary up_: a data field gets `Activity.Info` every second and can post duration, distance, averages and zone times (under 1 KB) when the ride ends. _Workouts down_: `makeWebRequest` with the FIT content type stores a workout on the Edge (520 Plus, 530, 830, 1030, 540, 840, 1040, 1050; not the 130 Plus or the Explore), and Kadence writes FIT workouts already. GarminHomeAssistant (MIT, in the store) shows the pattern of an app that posts to the user's own server from a URL and key in its settings.
- **Conditions.** The developer must be 18 and publish a privacy policy; review normally takes 72 hours; the store is the realistic channel, since sideloading needs a computer. Edge firmware 31.30 left Connect IQ fields crashing on the 540, 840 and 1040, unresolved on 2026-09-25.

It complements whichever route brings the file; it does not replace it.

- **Karoo is the most open.** Its developer account is created by the developer, who defines the app and then must "Accept the API Licensing agreement"; no review is described. The interface matches Kadence's `DeviceProvider` as it is: OAuth, a signed webhook, a file to fetch, a workout to push. It is also an Android device, so a sideloaded app can read the `FitFiles/` folder and post to a team's own server. Costs: SRAM may revoke at will, data passes through the United States, and "We do not support RPE-based workouts".
- **COROS is unclear.** The MCP article says "No application or approval required" and also "No two-way activity sync (COROS to your platform requires Partner API)". Whether pulling every ride into a coaching platform through MCP is meant to be allowed is a question for COROS. Its single scope also covers sleep, HRV and cycle data. Reviewers report power and cadence dropouts on the DURA.
- **Sigma is the cleanest and the least automatic**: no cloud, no account, a cable.
- **No device sends a ride to an address of the rider's choosing** (**second round**, the help pages of eleven makers). Wahoo comes nearest: besides Dropbox it can "auto-email yourself your workout as a .FIT file upon workout completion"; Kadence takes no mail. On a Karoo, riders sideload Syncthing and carry the files to a computer ("I sync with syncthing to Golden cheetah which works very well", 2022 and again 2026); an extension that fires a webhook at the end of a ride exists, without the file.
- **Phones and home-built units** (OpenTracks, pizero_bikecomputer) are not fit for a teenager racing on the road.
- **Switching has a price.** DKK 800–3,300 for a second computer, and the rider leaves Garmin's training status, the watch that syncs with it, and a club where everybody knows Garmin. Radar is not lost. The cheapest switch is none: keep the Garmin.

## 7. What Kadence has and what each route needs

_As the code stood on the morning of 2026-09-29, before ADR 0029. The first, fourth and fifth rows of the table are built since; the others are not._

Today a ride enters in two ways: a signed-in upload (`src/app/api/activities/upload/route.ts`) or a provider's notification (`src/modules/devices/webhooks.ts`). Both end in `importFitIn` (`src/modules/devices/import.ts`), which reads every format, recognises a ride it has (by sha256 and by start second), stores the original and matches it to the day's planned session. **No route accepts a file without a session cookie**, and there is no token table.

## 9. Recommendation

1. **Build the door first: an upload key per rider** (built the same day: ADR 0029, docs/upload-keys.md). A key the rider makes under Settings → Devices, shown once, stored as a hash, good for one thing: handing a ride file to that rider. One route takes a file with the key, finds the rider through a definer as `device_link_owner` does, and imports as the rider under the ordinary policies. With it come the folder picker ("Hent fra enheden"), the shared file on Android, and "last ride received, and from where" so that silence is seen. Nobody's approval is needed, it works with the internet cut, and every other route in this note ends at this door.
2. **A relay adapter as the automatic path for the rider with an Edge.** Tredict first, because its terms admit the riders Kadence is for and its servers are in the EU; intervals.icu beside it for riders of 18 and over, and for everyone if intervals.icu says in writing that a minor with a guardian's consent is welcome. Both are optional, chosen by the rider, named in `docs/subprocessors.md`, and show "Garmin" with the device model on the rides they bring. This needs a scheduler, which is an architectural decision of its own, and written answers before a line is built (below). **Answered:** both relays take the rider's own key, which needs no client of Kadence's and no keys on the installation; Tredict's is the one to build first.
3. **Send the Data Act request now.** One rider, with a parent, names Kadence's operator as the third party under art. 5(1) and asks for the feed existing partners get. It costs a letter; the answer, or the lack of one, goes to Digitaliseringsstyrelsen.
4. **A Karoo adapter when a rider has one, and the Wahoo application sent**, which `docs/launch.md` already lists as Martin's. To parents who ask what to buy: the Garmin you have is fine; bought new, a Wahoo or a Karoo brings the ride by itself, and a Sigma does it by cable with no account at all.
5. **Gadgetbridge as a written recipe** for the Android family that wants no cloud in between, pointed at the upload key, and a target offered to Gadgetbridge once its general upload layer lands.
6. **Connect IQ later, and for the other direction**: today's session on the Edge. The smallest experiment is ten rides with a sideloaded field on an Edge 530 and an 840, one iPhone and one Android.
7. **Not the unofficial login**, neither shipped nor described by Kadence. The upload key lets a parent run whatever they choose on their own machine; that is their decision and their account, and Kadence neither asks for a Garmin password nor holds a Garmin token.

**What only Martin can do, and can do this week:**

- Ask the winter's team what they ride and carry: Edge model, phone, and whether anyone uses Tredict or intervals.icu already. An Edge 530 and an Android phone make the cable enough; an Edge 840 and an iPhone leave the relay as the only automatic path.
- Write to Tredict (support@tredict.com): an OAuth client for Kadence, whether the file download works for a rider without paid access, and whether each self-hosted installation may have its own client. _Done and answered; the reply asks the four questions that were left._
- Apply for an intervals.icu OAuth app, and ask about riders under 18 with a guardian's consent. _Asked and answered; no application is needed for the rider's own key._
- Send the Data Act request with one rider and a parent: to Garmin Würzburg GmbH, from the person who uses the device, citing that Garmin's notice does not say how such a request is made (art. 3(3)(f)).
- Send the Wahoo application, and ask in it whether heart rate is passed on for riders under 16.

**Uncertainties, stated plainly:** whether Tredict holds Garmin's written approval for passing rides on (intervals.icu says Garmin asks for attribution only); whether Tredict's file download needs paid access, which its terms since June 2026 make likely; whether Hammerhead's keys and COROS's self-registration really work without a review, and for how many riders (no account was registered); whether COROS allows MCP as a feed to a coaching platform; which Edges Gadgetbridge really serves, the 530, 830, 540 and 840 above all; whether a plain-storage Edge mounts on an iPhone, which is reported for an iPad only; who the "user" is when a parent bought the device a rider uses; whether Garmin's account pages hide a Data Act request for third parties (they could not be reached); what Garmin's reopened programme will ask, and when. The Danish prices are one day's reading.

## Dansk sammenfatning til træneren

Garmin har lukket for nye samarbejdspartnere siden marts 2026 og købte TrainingPeaks i juli, så den officielle vej til automatisk overførsel er ikke åben for Kadence lige nu. De værktøjer, der alligevel henter ture automatisk uden aftale med Garmin, gør det ved at logge ind med rytterens Garmin-adgangskode. Det er i strid med Garmins vilkår, det var nede i op til to måneder sidste vinter, Garmin har låst konti på grund af det, og det giver adgang til hele rytterens Garmin-konto, også søvn og puls i hvile. Det vil vi ikke bygge på med sundhedsdata om unge. Der er to veje, som holder. Den ene er et mellemled: rytteren forbinder én gang sin Garmin til en tjeneste, der allerede har aftale med Garmin og giver den originale fil videre, og derefter kommer turen af sig selv, også på iPhone. Tredict er tysk, ligger i EU og er for ryttere fra 14 år, men kan komme til at koste rytteren omkring 49 dollar om året; intervals.icu er gratis, men er efter egne vilkår ikke for unge under 18. Begge har svaret skriftligt: hos Tredict laver rytteren selv en nøgle og sætter den ind i Kadence, og intervals.icu tilbydes kun ryttere, der er fyldt 18. Den anden vej er kablet: sæt cykelcomputeren til telefonen eller computeren, åbn Kadence, tryk én gang, og alle nye ture kommer med. Det virker på de ældre Edge-modeller fra telefonen og på de nyere fra en computer; det er bygget, men endnu ikke prøvet med en rigtig cykelcomputer i hånden. Hver rytter kan også lave en personlig upload-nøgle, som et program på familiens computer kan aflevere ture med. Imens sender vi Garmin en anmodning efter EU's dataforordning, som giver rytteren ret til at få sine data sendt til en tjeneste, de selv vælger. Til forældre, der skal købe ny cykelcomputer: den Garmin, I har, er fin. Køber I nyt, kan en Wahoo eller en Hammerhead Karoo sende turen til Kadence af sig selv, og en Sigma gør det med et kabel helt uden konto.
